Overview

We integrate sensing, estimation and control on a simulated TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi in MuJoCo. The robot starts in one corner of a 4 m × 4 m room with four obstacles, follows a planned path past them, and docks at a charging station marked with an ArUco marker.

The robot estimates its pose with an extended Kalman filter that fuses wheel-encoder odometry with range, bearing and heading measured from the ArUco marker. It follows the path with a gain-scheduled LQR tracker that uses only the estimated state, never the simulator's ground truth. A state machine handles the docking approach, marker re-acquisition and recovery from disturbances.

Changes to the supplied robot model:

  • The chassis collision mesh touched the floor and dragged with full friction, so the robot reached only about 50% of the commanded speed and could not turn in place. The chassis contact is now frictionless, so it behaves like the real ball casters.
  • The wheel motor gain was raised from 0.1 to 1. With 0.1 the wheels reached only about 75% of the commanded speed because of joint friction. With 1 they reach about 97%.
  • The model had no camera, LiDAR or encoders, so these were added at the positions of the real Raspberry Pi camera, LDS-01 and Dynamixel motors.

Model

State $x = [p_x, p_y, \theta]^T$ of {B} in {W}, input $u = [v, \omega]^T$. Differential-drive (unicycle) kinematics with wheel radius r = 0.033 m and wheel separation b = 0.288 m:

$$\dot p_x = v\cos\theta,\quad \dot p_y = v\sin\theta,\quad \dot\theta = \omega,\qquad v = \tfrac{r(\omega_R+\omega_L)}{2},\quad \omega = \tfrac{r(\omega_R-\omega_L)}{b}$$

Assumptions: no wheel slip, flat floor, motor dynamics fast compared with the controller. Checked in simulation: commanding 0.15 m/s gives 0.146 m/s, and commanding 0.5 rad/s gives 0.474 rad/s, with wheel speeds matching the kinematics exactly.

Discrete-time model for the estimator, a midpoint step on the encoder wheel-angle changes $\Delta\phi_L, \Delta\phi_R$ over each 20 ms step:

$$\Delta s = \tfrac{r(\Delta\phi_R+\Delta\phi_L)}{2},\quad \Delta\theta = \tfrac{r(\Delta\phi_R-\Delta\phi_L)}{b},\quad \theta_m = \theta_k + \tfrac{\Delta\theta}{2}$$

$$x_{k+1} = x_k + \begin{bmatrix} \Delta s\cos\theta_m \\ \Delta s\sin\theta_m \\ \Delta\theta \end{bmatrix},\qquad F_k = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -\Delta s\sin\theta_m \\ 0 & 1 & \Delta s\cos\theta_m \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Tracking-error model for the controller, linearised about a reference $(v_r, \omega_r)$, with the error in the robot frame:

$$\dot e = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \omega_r & 0 \\ -\omega_r & 0 & v_r \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} e + \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \delta u$$

Marker measurement and frames

Each detection gives $z = [\text{range}, \text{bearing}, \text{robot heading}]$. Range $= \lVert t \rVert$ and bearing $= -\operatorname{atan2}(t_x, t_z)$ come from the solvePnP translation $t$. Heading comes from the full marker pose, chained through the frames:

$$T_{W,B} = T_{W,M}\, T_{C,M}^{-1}\, T_{B,C}^{-1}$$

  • World {W}: x east, y north, z up.
  • Base {B}: x forward, y left, origin at the wheel axle.
  • Camera {C}: OpenCV convention, x right, y down, z forward; 0.086 m ahead of and 0.107 m above the base.
  • Marker {M}: x right, y up, z out of the face.

The camera transform was checked numerically against MuJoCo's camera pose.

Calibration

The camera measurement was compared against simulator ground truth at 400 random poses facing the marker, 0.45 to 4.2 m away (analysis/marker_noise.py). Ground truth is used only for this offline calibration, never at run time.

QuantityBiasNoise (std)Used in the EKF
Rangereads long by 0.0088 r²0.0042 r²bias removed (k = 0.009), σ = 0.005 r²
Bearing+0.056°0.022°σ = 0.1°
Heading from PnPnone0.94° per m (robust); 11% off by more than 10°σ = max(1°, 0.9° per m), separate gate
  • Range error grows with r² because range comes from the marker's size in pixels ($w = fL/r$). The fit corresponds to a corner error of about 0.33 px.
  • The bearing offset is half a pixel: the principal point is at 320 px instead of 319.5 px.
  • Heading suffers from the planar PnP ambiguity, which causes 10° to 30° outliers. The EKF gate rejects them.
  • Noise handling: unknown marker ids are ignored (floor reflections were once decoded as id 17), and non-finite PnP results are dropped before the filter.

The process noise is built from the wheels, where the noise actually enters:

$$Q_k = G_k J\, \Sigma_w\, J^T G_k^T,\qquad \Sigma_w = \operatorname{diag}\big((0.02|\Delta\phi_L| + 10^{-4})^2,\ (0.02|\Delta\phi_R| + 10^{-4})^2\big)$$

The measurement model predicts range and bearing from the camera position $c = p + 0.086\,[\cos\theta, \sin\theta]^T$ to the known marker position $m$, and heading directly:

$$h(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \lVert m - c \rVert \\ \operatorname{atan2}(m_y - c_y,\ m_x - c_x) - \theta \end{bmatrix},\qquad h_\theta(x) = \theta$$

Range and bearing are accurate and heading is not, so they are updated separately. A rejected heading never throws away a good range and bearing.

Navigation and control

Path

Six hand-placed waypoints are joined by circular fillets (R = 0.30 m), so the robot never stops to turn on the spot, which is the uncontrollable case ($v_r = 0$). The path stays at least 0.34 m from every obstacle and ends with a straight 0.4 m approach along y = 1.2 into the docking point (1.70, 1.20), facing +x. Speed is 0.15 m/s, dropping to 0.08 m/s for the last 0.5 m, with 0.10 m/s² acceleration and braking. Length 5.10 m, duration 38.1 s.

Controller

The tracking error $e$ (along-track, lateral, heading) is computed in the robot frame from the EKF estimate only. The command is feedforward from the reference plus an LQR correction:

$$v = v_r\cos e_3 + K_1 e,\qquad \omega = \omega_r + K_2 e$$

ItemValue
Q, R (Bryson's rule)Q = 1/(0.10 m, 0.05 m, 10°)², R = 1/(0.10 m/s, 0.50 rad/s)²
Gain schedulingdiscrete Riccati equation re-solved every 20 ms for the current $(v_r, \omega_r)$, doubling algorithm, under 1 ms
Example gain (straight, 0.15 m/s)K = [[0.99, 0, 0], [0, 9.67, 3.27]]
Linearisation floor$v_r \ge 0.05$ m/s in the model, because lateral error is uncontrollable at $v_r = 0$
Wheel saturationv and ω scaled together above 7.88 rad/s per wheel, keeping the path curvature

Lateral error is weighted most because it decides collisions and docking.

LiDAR

Forward speed is capped by the closest return within ±30° of straight ahead: no cap above 0.35 m, falling to zero at 0.18 m (0.25 m and 0.12 m while docking, where the dock wall is expected). The LiDAR only slows the robot and never steers.

Tracking results

Closed loop, nominal: lateral RMSE 5 mm (max 9 mm), heading RMSE 0.23°, docking error 4 mm.

Diagnosed during tuning: a first LiDAR cap at the cruise speed stopped the controller catching up for the wheels' 3% speed loss, so along-track error grew to 6 cm. Capping at the robot's top speed (0.26 m/s) brought it down to 0.9 cm.

Trade-offs

From analysis/sweeps.py, 3 seeds per setting. All 39 runs docked.

  • Control effort against accuracy. Lowering the LQR effort weight from R × 4 to R × 0.25 cuts docking error from 7.2 mm to 2.6 mm, but nearly doubles the speed correction (0.0048 to 0.0091 m/s) and raises the peak wheel speed from 7.14 to 7.58 rad/s (limit 7.88). Lateral tracking does not change, because the remaining true error is estimation error.
  • Consistency against caution. NEES crosses the ideal 3 between R inflation 2× and 3× (used: 3×, NEES 2.43). Under a 1% encoder bias, raising the wheel noise k from 0.02 to 0.05 brings NEES from 14.1 to 3.49 and removes the need to relocalise, with no loss of accuracy. We kept k = 0.02 and report 0.05 as the better setting for a robot with uncalibrated wheels.

Limitations

  • 10 trials per scenario show the behaviour is repeatable, but cannot rule out a failure rate of about 10%.
  • The nominal docking error is a repeatable 3.9 mm short of the target: the robot stops slightly early as the reference slows to zero. The LQR sweep traces this to the controller's effort weight.
  • There is one marker. Until it is in view (about the first 10 s) the robot runs on odometry alone, and heading is only weakly constrained at long range.
  • The process noise models random wheel noise only. Under encoder bias or a push the filter is overconfident (NEES 14 to 67) until the camera corrects it. Relocalising after 10 rejections is a rule of thumb, not a model of the disturbance.
  • The path is hand-planned on a known map. The LiDAR slows or stops the robot but does not re-plan around unknown obstacles.
  • The last 0.3 m is driven without the marker, which no longer fits in the camera image.
  • The simulation is idealised: odometry drifts about 11 mm over 7 m, and there is no camera latency or motion blur. The 3× measurement-noise inflation was tuned on one path.
  • Results vary by about a millimetre between machines, because camera rendering and OpenCV builds differ slightly.
